Check an email for warning signs before you act on it.

Paste a suspicious message and see every red flag, exactly how much it counts, and what to do next. This is an educational triage tool: it points out warning signs, but it can’t prove whether an email is safe or malicious.

Email to analyse

Headers reveal the real sender and whether the email passed SPF, DKIM and DMARC checks. Copy the “original” or “source” view and paste all of it:

  • Gmail (web): open the email, click the three-dot menu, choose Show original, then Copy to clipboard.
  • Outlook (web): three-dot menu → View → View message source.
  • Outlook (Windows): open the email → File → Properties → Internet headers, then paste the body below them.
  • Apple Mail: View → Message → Raw Source.

Copying the source never opens links or attachments, so it is a safe way to inspect a message.

Stay safe while you check

Don’t click to test it

Opening a suspicious link or attachment “just to see” is exactly what the attacker wants. Paste the text here instead.

Verify through a channel you trust

Contact the organisation using its official website, its app, or a phone number you already know — never details from the email itself.

Already clicked or replied?

Change the password from the official site, turn on multi-factor authentication, and call your bank if money or card details were involved. Report it to your IT team or email provider. In Canada you can also report fraud to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.

How the score works

There is no hidden formula. Each warning sign below adds a fixed number of points, once, however many times it appears. The points are added up and capped at 100.

Low Risk0–24
Suspicious25–59
High Risk60–100

Scores from 0 to 24 are Low Risk, 25 to 59 are Suspicious, and 60 to 100 are High Risk.

Passing SPF, DKIM or DMARC never lowers the score: attackers can register their own domains and pass all three. Those checks only prove which domain sent the message, not that the sender is honest.

Your email stays on your device

All analysis happens inside this browser tab, using code you can read. The text you paste is never uploaded to a server, an AI service, an analytics provider or any other third party.

  • No accounts, cookies, tracking or analytics.
  • Nothing is saved — close or refresh the tab and the email is gone.
  • The page loads no fonts, scripts or images from other websites.
  • A browser security policy blocks this page from making network requests at all.
  • Links found in your email are shown as plain text and can’t be clicked.

What this tool can’t do

  • It can’t tell you for certain that an email is safe or malicious. A careful attacker can avoid every sign listed here.
  • It doesn’t visit links, open attachments or check reputation databases.
  • When you copy an email as plain text, the real address behind a “Click here” link is usually lost. Paste the source view to detect misleading links.
  • Authentication results are only meaningful when they come from your own mail provider’s headers, not from text inside the message.
